# Round 5 / ECDLP-13 pre-registered protocol (RUN-62168bb1-0f58-4712-b4b2-e6a2774fc310)

Written BEFORE running round5.py. Owner: discovery agent. Toy sizes only; no asymptotic claim.

Question: does a factor base V = {Q != O : x(Q) in S}, with S an algebraically defined set other than small-x,
behave differently from a random negation-closed point set of equal size (m = 2), and can a decomposition of a random R
be FOUND faster than by scanning V?

Domain: k in {12,14,16,18}; per k, 3 primes p in [0.9*2^k,1.1*2^k] found by seeded search with a divisor h of p-1 in
[0.8*sqrt(p), 1.25*sqrt(p)]; per prime 2 curves y^2=x^3+ax+b (a,b uniform; nonsingular). Master seed 20261103,
random.Random(f"20261103-{k}-{idx}"). #E by square-count table (exact).
Families (all negation-closed; n = |V| signed points; all truncated/selected so n ~ n_H):
 F1  H: x in H, H the order-h subgroup of F_p^* (PKM-2016 instance 1 shape, smoothness of h NOT imposed here)
 F1c random coset aH (a uniform), 4 per curve
 F4a x = t^2 mod p, t = 1.. (image of a degree-2 map on small t; n matched by taking t until n points)
 F4b x = 1/t mod p, t = 1.. (rational map of degree 1)
 SX  small x (x < B, B minimal with n >= n_H)  [prior-round baseline family]
 RND random x-set, 5 draws per curve (baseline); plus analytic baseline n^2/(2 #E).
Measurement 1 (exact): enumerate all ordered pairs of V (n^2); pi = |{P1+P2}|/#E (V symmetric, so V+V = V-V);
additive energy ratio e = sum_R mult(R)^2 / (n^4/#E) over distinct sums R (collision structure). Per family, average over
curves; report ratio pi_family / mean pi_RND per curve and its mean/min/max.
Measurement 2 (cost of finding): 200 random R per curve (1 curve per k). Method SCAN: for each x1 in S (generated by one
multiplication per step for H) solve the Semaev quadratic S_3(x1,x2,x(R))=0 in x2 (coefficients explicit) and test x2 in S.
Cost units = field multiplications: 8 for coefficients, 1.5*log2(p) for the square-root/Legendre, and membership test either
by exponentiation x2^h==1 (1.5*log2 h) or by hash lookup (0). Also METHOD TABLE: all unordered pair-sums precomputed (n^2/2 ops build),
per R one lookup. Verification: every SCAN decomposition checked by the group law; SCAN success rate vs Measurement 1 pi.
Sub-scan test (structure of covered set; AMENDED before running, since pooled exponents of all pairs are uniform by
construction and would be uninformative): for the H family, let d=(p-1)/h and cls(x)=x^h mod p (coset of H containing x,
d classes). Compare, over classes, the number of covered R x-values (x(R) for R in V+V) against the number of all curve x-values
in the class: statistic chi2 of covered counts vs expected (total covered * all_in_class/all), z=(chi2-df)/sqrt(2 df), df=#classes-1
(classes with expected <1 pooled away: skipped, count reported). Null calibration: same statistic for the RND family. If x(R) of a
relation had to lie in a restricted set of cosets, |z_H| would be large and membership of R in V+V could be pre-screened.
Pre-registered predictions: (P1) pi_F1 / pi_RND mean in [0.9,1.1] at k=18 for H and cosets; F4a,F4b,SX likewise. (P2) energy ratio
in [0.9,1.3]. (P3) |z_H|<3 and within the RND null spread (mean+-3 sd over RND draws). (P4) SCAN ops per R ~ n*(8+1.5log2 p)(+exp) i.e. linear in n; no implemented
method below n/4 units per R.
Decision rule: a structured family is "interesting" iff (a) pi ratio > 1.2 with min over curves > 1.05 at k=18, OR (b) an
implemented, correctness-checked decomposition method uses < n/4 field-mult-units per R on average at k=18. If neither:
no evidence of advantage over small-x/random on the tested domain (does not prove anything asymptotically).
Runtime limit 5 min; reduce curves if exceeded and say so.
