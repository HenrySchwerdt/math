## 2. Item 1: mechanism (what I tried, and what the exact computations say)

### (i) Parametrising the decomposition variety
Fix R with x(R) = x3. S_3 = (x1-x2)^2 x3^2 - 2((x1+x2)(x1x2+a)+2b) x3 + (x1x2-a)^2 - 4b(x1+x2) has bidegree (2,2) in (x1,x2).
**Exact fact (e1, sympy):** disc_{x2} S_3 = 16 f(x1) f(x3), f = x^3+ax+b. So C_R: S_3(x1,x2,x3)=0 is a double cover of the x1-line branched at the 3 roots of f and at infinity, i.e. w^2 = f(x1) (times a constant): genus 1, and the j-invariant of the quartic model equals j(E) identically (invariants I,J computed symbolically: difference simplifies to 0). The map P -> (x(P), x(R-P)) is the parametrisation: C_R is birational to E.
**Brute-force confirmation (e1, 15 curves, p = 101..2053):** #{(x1,x2) in F_p^2 : S_3 = 0} = #E(F_p) - 2 = |{(x(P), x(R-P))}| in every case; every pair with both x in F_p comes from a rational P (a pair from the quadratic twist would force 2R = 0, excluded for odd-order R).
So the "rational/low-genus parametrisation" **exists and is free**: it is the group law. It does not help, because the difficulty is not producing points of C_R but making both coordinates land in a small factor base V.

Factor base given as an image x = phi(t). The relation set becomes the fibre-product curve Y: S_3(phi(t1), phi(t2), x3) = 0 of bidegree (2e, 2e), e = deg phi. Genus computed exactly (e2: Groebner/Tjurina numbers in all four charts of P^1 x P^1 over Q; Riemann-Hurwitz independently):

| phi | e | arithmetic genus (2e-1)^2 | singularities found | genus of Y |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| t (identity) | 1 | 1 | none | 1 |
| t^2 | 2 | 9 | 4 nodes (Tjurina 1 each) at t=inf | **5** |
| t^3 | 3 | 25 | 6 cusps (Tjurina 2 each) at t=inf | **19** |
| (t^2+1)/(t^2+2t+3), inf unramified | 2 | 9 | none (smooth) | **9** |

Why the monomial/polynomial case has singularities: the x1-projection of C_R is ramified over x1 = infinity (P = O), and t -> t^e is totally ramified there, so the fibre product is locally w^2 = s^e (2e such points, delta = floor(e/2) each). General formulas (Riemann-Hurwitz, matching the table): generic phi with infinity unramified: g = (2e-1)^2; phi = t^e (the PKM multiplicative coset x = g t^e): g = (3e-1)(e-1) for e even, 3e^2-3e+1 for e odd. Not computed for e >= 4 symbolically (Groebner too slow in sympy; e = 3 rational case timed out), so e >= 4 rests on the formula.
The only phi with g <= 1 is e = 1 (Moebius), whose image is all of F_p.
A base of size s = |phi(F_p)| needs e >= ~p/s (fibres have <= e points). The relevant regime is s >= sqrt(p) (see supply bound), hence **e >= sqrt(p) and g >= ~3p to 4p** (3e^2 or 4e^2). The Weil window p + 1 +- 2g sqrt(p) is then empty of information (e2 table (c): at p = 1009, e = 6 the window half-width is 5400 > p). Y(F_p) has ~p points regardless (measured |N - p|/sqrt p between 0 and 10.7 across e = 1..6, p = 1009..6007), and the fraction in V^2 is 1/e^2. Producing them needs no better than enumeration: there is no group law on Y, and Y is a (Z/e)^2 Kummer cover of an elliptic curve whose F_p-count is a sum of (e-1)^2 twisted character sums (the sums of HYP-80158648), each equidistributed-looking.
Choosing a base from a curve C with psi: C -> E of degree e: Riemann-Hurwitz gives g(Y) = 2e(gamma-1)+1 (derived, not computationally verified), >= 2e+1 for gamma >= 2; gamma = 1 is an isogeny (see (ii)).

### (ii) CM / isogeny orbits
(a) Isogeny orbit. For a rational isogeny psi: E' -> E of degree l != r with #E(F_p) = r prime: #E'(F_p) = r (Tate), ker psi has no F_p-point (order coprime to r), so psi is an isomorphism on F_p-points. The "orbit base" of a point is a subset of the same cyclic group in a different coordinate function of degree 2l. S_3^{E'} on it is the same genus-1 curve (e1 argument applied to E'). No new structure; this is the PKM isogeny-coset idea (D, ECDLP-43). Consistent with the lemma HYP-caa15771.
(b) Endomorphism orbit, j = 0 (e4). Exact factorisation of S_3 on the line (x, zeta x, z): the z^2-coefficient is a constant times x^2; one root is z = zeta^2 x identically (S_3(x, zeta x, zeta^2 x) = 0, the identity P + wP = -w^2 P, i.e. 1+w+w^2 = 0); the other root is z2 = x((1-w)P) = c (x^3+4b)/x^2 (degree (3,2); sympy, a constant c in Q(sqrt(-3))). So S_3 does factor on an orbit, but into the **trivial identities of Z[w]**.
On E(F_p) = <P0>, w acts as a scalar mu with mu^2+mu+1 = 0 mod r (computable without DLP). A base V_B = {x(alpha P0): alpha = a + b w, |a|,|b| <= B} has **known logs** a + b mu. Relations inside V_B (p = 6229 and 28729, r = 6277 and 28759):
| B | |V| | distinct triples observed | of which Z[w]-identities | remainder |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 40 | 288 | 284 | 4 |
| 8 | 144 | 3845 | 3832 | 13 |
| 16 | 544 | 55328 | 55280 | 48 |
(rel_obs/3, since the counter sees each triple once per pair; both p give identical rows because no lattice relation has appeared yet.) The remainder is far below the random-set count at the same size (e.g. 282 distinct triples at B = 8, r = 6277), and lattice relations (sum vector in L = {(a,b): a+b mu = 0 mod r}, shortest vector 70.6 and 139.6, i.e. 0.89 sqrt(r) and 0.82 sqrt(r)) cannot occur until 3B*sqrt(2) ~ lambda_1, i.e. B ~ 0.2 sqrt(r), |V| ~ 2B^2 ~ 0.08 r. So CM yields exactly the identities that carry no information, and the base is a 2-D box of known multiples of P0, i.e. a baby-step table: DLP then needs a target in V+V, a table lookup (BSGS/GLV-type) with no advantage over 1-D BSGS. This is the known GLV/automorphism constant-factor saving (ECDLP-43), not an exponent change.

### (iii) Any other construction: the supply bound (finder-independent)
The finder is not the only bottleneck. To solve for s unknown logs one needs about s-1 relations among them. With S_3 (3 points) a relation among base elements is a closed triple (x1,x2,x3 all in V). Measured (e3; random-curve-point bases and the PKM coset base, p = 12289 and 61441): let N3 = number of ordered (x1,x2) in V^2 with x(P1 +- P2) in V.
- N3 / (4 s^3 / p) = 0.98 (coset 514 elements, p = 12289), 0.985 (coset 1016, p = 61441), 0.98 and 1.03 (random bases, s ~ 2 sqrt p); same for coset and random bases.
- mean target yield: H over curve x-values = 15.6 vs 4s^2/p = 15.9 (random, s=221), 84.6 vs 86.0 (coset, s=514); H/mean variance ~ 1.9-2.0 = ordered double counting of unordered pairs {x1,x2}; so **unordered relations per target = 2 s^2/p** (Poisson-like).
- distinct closed triples = N3/6 = 2 s^3/(3p).
Requiring >= s-1 closed triples gives **s >= sqrt(3p/2) ~ 1.22 sqrt(p)**. Any index calculus built on S_3 relations over a base that is random-like for S_3 incidences therefore costs >= s >= 1.22 sqrt(p) operations even with a finder that costs O(1) per relation, versus rho at ~0.89 sqrt(r) (with negation) or 1.25 sqrt(r): **S_3 cannot give an exponent below 1/2 whatever the finder**. The only escape is a base with more additive closure than random, and the only closure found is the Z[w]-identity set above (known logs, no unknowns to solve).
Consequence for the round 13 question: a finder cheaper than sqrt(search space) would at best convert D's total ~0.45p into ~1.2 sqrt(p) (the supply floor), matching rho's exponent. For m >= 3 (S_4, ...) the supply floor drops to s >= p^{1/m} (heuristic), which is where an improvement could still live; the decomposition variety S_4(x1,x2,x3,x_T)=0 is birational to E x E (abelian surface, Kodaira dimension 0, so not rational; derived, not computed here) and the same "parametrisation is the group law" obstacle applies. Not tested.

## 3. Item 2: pseudocode / prototype
Best candidate, "parametric finder" (the only way (i) produces points, and the same operation count as D's F1/D2):
```
input: V (sorted set of x-values with chosen y), target R=(xR,yR)
for each v=(x1,y1) in V, sign in {+1,-1}:       # s iterations, one inversion each
    Q = R - sign*v                                # point on C_R: (x1, x(Q)) satisfies S_3
    if x(Q) in V (hash):  emit relation  R = sign*v + Q
```
Correctness of the output: each emitted pair satisfies P1 + P2 = R (group identity; e1 shows the pair set equals the S_3 solution set). Expected yield per target 2 s^2/p (e3). No new code was benchmarked: it is D's `Finder` loop, so I do not re-measure. Total model: per-relation cost p/(2s); relations needed ~0.9 s (D); total ~ 0.45 p, matching D's measured 0.38-0.48 p (D2a, 16-24 bits). No prototype below sqrt(r) exists to build.

## 7. Item 6: exact obstacle
1. C_R has genus exactly 1 and is birational to E: the parametrisation is the group law, so there is nothing "rational" to exploit; the relation condition is a membership test x(R - P) in V.
2. Pullback to any base x = phi(t) with |phi(F_p)| <= p/e has genus (2e-1)^2 (infinity unramified) or (3e-1)(e-1) / 3e^2-3e+1 (monomial); with e >= sqrt(p) the genus is >= 3p, beyond the range where Weil gives anything. Genus <= 1 forces e = 1 (full F_p).
3. Rational isogenies and CM endomorphisms act on a prime-order group as scalars, so orbits have known logs; the S_3 factorisation on an orbit is the Z[w] identity.
4. Independent of any finder: S_3 relation supply is 2 s^3/(3p) closed triples, forcing s >= 1.22 sqrt(p) and total cost >= 1.22 sqrt(p).
