## Sources (V = verified by fetching the page/PDF in this run; level stated; U = unverified recollection)
1. V (abstract page). I. Semaev, "Summation polynomials and the discrete logarithm problem on elliptic curves", IACR ePrint 2004/031 (2004; "submitted to Crypto 2004"). Claim: introduces summation polynomials S_n(x_1..x_n)=0 iff points with these x sum to O; the DLP reduces to finding BOUNDED solutions of explicit modular multivariate equations; CONDITIONAL result: an algorithm solving these in polynomial/low-exponential time would beat Pollard. No such algorithm is given. Via PKM (V): for K=F_p Semaev proposed V={x < p^{1/m}} and gave no relation-finding algorithm.
2. V (full text read, extracted from PDF). C. Petit, M. Kosters, A. Messeng, "Algebraic approaches for the elliptic curve discrete logarithm problem over prime fields", PKC 2016 (LNCS 9615, pp. 3-18), http://christophe.petit.web.ulb.be/files/16PKC_primeECDLP.pdf . Exact content: factor bases F={(x,y): L(x)=0}, L a large-degree rational map that is a composition of small-degree maps L_j; relations from S_{m+1}(x_1..x_m,X)=0 plus x_{i,j+1}=L_j(x_{i,j}), L_{n0}(x_{i,n0})=0 solved by Groebner bases (only method tried). Instance 1: p-1 has a smooth divisor prod p_i ~ p^{1/m}; V = subgroup (or coset) of F_p^* of that order, L_j=x^{p_j}. Instance 2: auxiliary curve E'/F_p with smooth-order subgroup H of order ~p^{1/m}; V = x-coordinates of a coset of H on E', L_j from Velu isogeny pieces. Heuristic yield (deg L)^m/(m! p) solutions per R; total cost P + (m! p/(deg L)^{m-1}) T + (deg L)^omega; beats sqrt(p) only if T < p^{1/2-1/m}. NIST P-224 (p-1 = 2^96 * smooth) satisfies the prerequisite for m>=3, B=2. Stated limits: "complexity of our algorithms remains an open problem"; "practical only for small parameters"; no proof of asymptotic improvement; systems were found a bit harder in prime than binary cases but easier than generic ones in small experiments (Magma).

## Proposals (F1..F5). Property / why helps / must prove / invalidation / cheapest test / relevant results
**F1. V = {Q: x(Q) in aH}, H <= F_p^* of order h ~ p^{1/m}** (this IS PKM instance 1; not new; testing its structure only).
- Property: aH is the root set of x^h=a^h; when h=prod p_i smooth it is a tower of low-degree maps x->x^{p_i}.
- Why: turns "x<B" (no algebraic description) into a low-degree system in tower variables; hope that the system over F_p is easier than generic.
- Prove: relation density (deg L)^m/(m! p) (needs equidistribution of sums of aH-points); a cost bound T for the system.
- Invalidates: sums of aH-points concentrate (energy blow-up); solving cost T >= |V|^{m/2}-type (my sketch, U: for m=2, Res_{x1}(S_3,x1^h-1) has degree ~2h in x2, so any resultant/Groebner route is Otilde(h) per R, i.e. >= |V|).
- Cheapest test: exact V+V coverage/additive energy vs random (done, below) and the cost of finding.
- Results: PKM 2016 [2]; needs smooth p-1 divisor, so NOT universal ECDLP.
**F2. V = x-coords of a coset of a smooth subgroup H of an auxiliary curve E'/F_p** (PKM instance 2; known).
- Property: x(V) = zeros of x-map composed of Velu isogeny pieces; works for every p. Why: universality; unrelated to E so no group-structure interaction with the target. Prove: relation density (sums on E of x-values coming from E' behave like random; E' unrelated to E so plausibly yes). Invalidates: same solving cost as F1; extra precomputation p^{1/m} for finding E'. Test: coverage as F1 but with x-set from E' (not run; expected random by the same argument; U). Results: [2].
**F3. V = {Q: x(Q) = f(t), t in [0,B)}, f low-degree rational** (PKM-style parametrisation; generalises small-x; f=t^2 and f=1/t tested).
- Property: S_3(f(t1),f(t2),X)=0 is a curve C_X of bidegree (2d,2d) in (t1,t2); relations = small integer points on C_X mod p (small-root problem).
- Why: Coppersmith/lattice methods find small roots of modular bivariate polynomials with a conjectured-heuristic bound; the relation count with B~p^{1/2} is O(1) so one would need to find ~1 root with t1,t2 ~ p^{1/2}.
- Prove: heuristic Coppersmith bivariate bound XY < p^{2/(3 delta)} (U, my recollection: needs delta=2d => XY < p^{1/3} for d=1), versus needed XY ~ p: gap of a power of p in the exponent, so small-root methods cannot reach the relation regime (U, derived from recollection, not verified).
- Invalidates: that gap; or higher m with smaller B per variable (bound per variable p^{1/(delta...)}) - open to check.
- Cheapest test: coverage vs random (done); solving test would be to run LLL on the 2-variable lattice at toy size (not done).
- Results: Semaev/PKM only implicitly; I found no verified source doing Coppersmith on S_3. Speculative.
**F4. j=0 (or 1728) curves with V closed under the CM automorphism: x in H with 3 | h**
- Property: [omega](x,y)=(omega x,y); one relation R=P1+P2 gives omega R = omega P1+omega P2 (and omega^2 R) at the same cost.
- Why: constant factor sqrt(3)-like speedup (symmetrised summation polynomials idea; see Semaev "symmetrized summation polynomials" with small torsion, U title only from search snippet). Prove: orbit relations independent mod r. Invalidates: not applicable to general curves; only a constant. Test: trivial invariance; not run. Not an asymptotic route.
**F5. V = x in H1 + H2 style "lattice-like" set (sum of two subgroups/arithmetic progressions)**: additive structure (generalised arithmetic progressions) lets S_3 be attacked by sieving on a lattice. Property: additive closure; Why: constrains x1,x2 to lattices of rank 2 -> lattice reduction. Prove: heuristic bound as in F3. Invalidates: additive energy of V no use for the elliptic group law (group law is not additive in x). Test: not run. Speculative; no source.

## Verdict
F1/F3 (and small-x) are indistinguishable from random sets of equal size for V+V coverage and additive energy at toy sizes; nothing predicts a better yield than n^2/(2#E). The decisive criterion (decomposition found faster than scanning V, ideally polylog) was NOT met: the best implemented procedure is O(|V|) per R, and the table method beats any scan for amortised cost. Structure helps only in the PKM way: it changes the SHAPE of the relation system (low-degree, tower variables), not its solution count; whether Groebner/resultant methods exploit it is exactly the open problem in PKM, unresolved here. Hypothesis HYP-db508348 is therefore not weakened or strengthened beyond: 'structured algebraic factor bases F1, F3 tested at m=2 show no yield advantage'. No proof status changes.
