## Per-hypothesis findings

### H1 — verdict: abstract lemma correct but trivial; application/"unification" is vacuous, and the pairing reading is wrong for the q-Frobenius (flaw in derivation)
Abstract part: correct. f(x0)=f(Fx0)=c f(x0) gives (1-c)f(x0)=0, invertible when c!=1 in the field F_r. It uses neither additivity of F or f nor the vector-space structure of V; only F(x0)=x0 and the identity at v=x0.
Application, which Frobenius/equivariance:
1. Define "equivariant" as Galois-equivariant: f(pi x)=sigma(f(x)) = f(x)^q (scalar c=q on mu_r). The MOV/Frey-Ruck functional x -> e(x,R) with R non-rational is NOT equivariant in this sense. For e(x,R)=e(x,R)^... use e(pi x,pi y)=e(x,y)^q and the adjoint: e(pi x,R)=e(x,(q/pi)R), so f is eigen-equivariant with scalar c equal to the eigenvalue of the Verschiebung on R. R in the pi-eigenvalue-q space gives c=1 (nontrivial on rational P); R rational (pi R=R) gives c=q and then f(P)=1 on rational P unless r | q-1. Toy evidence (h1_output.txt, 4 curves, embedding degree 2, r=13/11, p=103/131): for R- with pi R- = -R- = qR-, X=P+R-: f(pi X)=f(X) in both Weil and reduced Tate pairing, while f(X)^p != f(X) (zeta^2 != 1). So f(pi X)==f(X) True, f(pi X)==f(X)^p False. The rational auxiliary point R+ lies in the cyclic group <P> and e(P,R+)=1, as the lemma predicts for c=q.
2. So the nonzero MOV transfer exists exactly because the auxiliary point breaks Galois-equivariance (c=1 branch). The lemma's condition "r | q^j - 1" with j=k (pi^k trivial on E[r], c=q^k=1) is just the definition of the embedding degree, satisfied by construction for j=k and by choosing j. For j=1 the lemma does not apply to the pairing. Verified pi^2=id on E[r] in all four toy cases.
3. Anomalous attack: the map E(F_p)[p] -> F_p (formal-group log of a lift, after multiplying by #E) is not an algebraic map defined over F_p; Frobenius acts as the identity on its source E(F_p), so c=1 and the lemma yields nothing. "F_r-linear (indeed any additive) map into (F_p,+) needs r=p" is just Hom(Z/r,Z/p)=0, no Frobenius used.
4. In both cases the whole content is cyclic-group Hom counting (nonzero hom from Z/r into F_{q^k}^* needs r | q^k-1; into F_p^+ needs r=p). Verdict on "unification": true only in the vacuous sense that both conditions are order-divisibility conditions; the Frobenius-equivariance step adds nothing and misdescribes the pairing. Not a counterexample to the abstract statement.
Precise content that is true and non-vacuous (new statement N1 below): a Galois-equivariant (defined over F_q) transfer to mu_r kills rational r-torsion unless r | q-1.

### H2 — verdict: survived attempt (inconclusive as to falsity); bound true as proved but far from sharp

Boundary cases checked: q square (4 sqrt q integer; r > 4 sqrt q still strict so fine), q=2 (interval [0.17,5.83], no primes >5.66 dividing N<=5), r | q excluded, N=0 not in interval (N >= (sqrt q -1)^2 > 0), prime power vs prime irrelevant (only q enters). ### H3 — verdict: flaw in statement/derivation found (length "<=L" vs "=L" gap; counting bound enormously non-sharp; bit-cost corollary conditional on an unjustified model)
1. Count: step i has i choices for j, i for l, 4 signs => 4i^2; product 4^L(L!)^2 is correct for programs of length exactly L with output a_L (j=l, doubling, identity all already included; no change). It overcounts distinct values (h3_output.txt: L=2: 64 programs, 9 distinct outputs; L=3: 2304 vs 15; L=4: 147456 vs 25).
2. Gap: the claim speaks of "length <= L" but the count is for exactly L. Exact-length outputs are not monotone: S_0={P} is not a subset of S_1={0,+-2P} (P has no length-1 program). Elements reachable with length <= L form a union of S_0..S_L; the rigorous count is sum_{i<=L} 4^i (i!)^2 > 4^L(L!)^2 (e.g. L=1: 5 vs 4). The deduced "if 4^L(L!)^2 < r then some Q has no SLP of length <= L" is therefore not implied by the stated count. In every case computed the conclusion still holds (reachable sets are tiny), so this is a derivation gap, not a false conclusion.
3. Numbers: largest L with 4^L(L!)^2 < 2^256 is 28 (so length >= 29: claim consistent); for 2^521 it is 49 (length >= 50: consistent). The asymptotic log2 r/(2 log2 log2 r) equals 16.0 and 28.9 at these sizes, far below 29 and 50: the asymptotic is correct only as L ~ log r/(2 log log r)(1+o(1)) and converges slowly.
4. Much stronger trivial bound: every a_i is an integer multiple n_i P with |n_i| <= 2^i, so at most 2^{L+1}+1 residues are reachable and some Q needs L >= log2(r-1)-1 (255 for 2^256, 520 for 2^521). Exhaustive check L<=4: all residues mod r are covered only for r <= 3, 9, 15, 23 (L=1..4), versus the hypothesis thresholds 4, 64, 2304, 147456. The hypothesis is thus true but misleading: the true SLP lower bound is Omega(log r), and the counting argument loses a factor Theta(log log r).
5. Bit cost: "each group operation costs >= c*b" is not justified by anything stated; it is an assumption (black-box, separately implemented ops). A Boolean-circuit implementation of the whole SLP may share work across steps, so Omega(L*b) is not a theorem for general bit complexity. With n = Theta(b) the arithmetic Omega(b^2/log b)=omega(n log n) is correct but only inside the SLP/black-box model; verification by a non-SLP algorithm is unconstrained. Also needs instances with log r = Theta(b) (r could be tiny), and an SLP verifier is given the program non-uniformly (cost of finding it unaccounted). Verdict: the final "omega(n log n)" is a statement about a restricted model, not a verification lower bound.

### H4 — verdict: ill-posed quantifier; false for "arbitrary lifts" under the universal reading (consistent lifts), true under the independent-random-lift reading (exact, not just chance)
Setup: psi(P~) = -X/Y of [#E]P~ (projective, divided by p) mod p, computed in Z/p^3 with complete addition (odd #E, y!=0). Checked homomorphism on E~(Z_p): psi(P~+Q~)=psi(P~)+psi(Q~) in 1650/1650 trials; therefore psi([k]P~)=k psi(P~) exactly.
- T1 anomalous (#E=p, 42 curves, p in 101..): independent random lifts recover k in 504/504 informative trials (no degenerate-lift trials in the final run; in an earlier run some curve lifts had psi identically 0, giving 0=0, which is a lift property not a failure of the attack).
- T2 exhaustive: for 30 non-anomalous curves (p=11..23, r | #E, r != p, p not | #E), over all p lifts (mod p^2) of P and all p lifts of Q=kP, the map (lift of P, lift of Q) -> (psi(P~),psi(Q~)) is a bijection onto F_p^2. So with independent uniformly random lifts the joint law is exactly uniform and independent of k (provable: psi(P~+eps)=psi(P~)+#E z(eps)/p, eps in E_1 and p not | #E).
- T3 randomized, 1650 trials per category, average chance 1/p=0.0441: independent lifts 74 (0.0448); independent lifts and random curve lift 73 (0.0442); Teichmuller x-lifts 75 (0.0455) — chance.
- T4 consistent lifts Q~=[k]P~: 1650/1650 (rate 1; control, needs k). Q~=[k]P~+eps (eps in E_1 as difference of two lifts): 64/1650=0.039 (chance).
- T5 torsion (canonical) lift of order r: psi=0 on all 1650 (0=0, again no information).
- T6 case p | #E, r != p: exhaustive Hasse check shows for p>=7 only #E=p is possible (primes 7..199), so r=p is forced and the case is excluded by hypothesis; remaining instance p=5, #E=10 (only curve y^2=x^3+3x in this scan): psi is lift-independent and vanishes on the y!=0 points (note the addition law used is not guaranteed complete for even order, weak evidence), subgroup of order 2 gives only 0=0. p=3 (#E=6) not tested (toyec needs p>=5).
When the statement is false: (i) if "arbitrary lifts" includes lifts consistent with the group law (Q~=[k]P~), psi(Q~)=k psi(P~) holds with probability 1; psi carries information about k only given such a lift, which requires knowing k. (ii) r=p (excluded). (iii) It is also vacuous where psi is identically 0 (torsion lifts, bad curve lifts).

## New corrected statements (separate; originals unchanged)
N1 (from H1, true, trivial): Let f: E[r] -> mu_r be defined over F_q, i.e. f(pi x)=f(x)^q for all x. Then f(x0)^{q-1}=1 for rational x0, hence f(x0)=1 unless r | q-1. Pairing functionals e(.,R) with non-rational R are not of this type; they satisfy f(pi x)=f(x)^c with c the Verschiebung eigenvalue of R (c=1 for pi R=qR).
N2 (from H2, provable): for every prime power q and M>=1 the number of M-bad t is at most 2*sum_{m<=M} phi(m) - M (M=1: 1; M=2: 2; M=3: 5).
N3 (from H3, provable): with all L-step addition/subtraction SLPs on P, every output is n P with |n|<=2^L, so if r > 2^{L+1}+1 some Q in <P> has no SLP of length <= L. Also, correct count for "length <= L": at most sum_{i=0}^{L} 4^i (i!)^2 outputs.
N4 (from H4, exact): Let E/F_p, p not | #E, fixed curve lift mod p^2 and P of order r != p. For independent uniform lifts of P and kP mod p^2, (psi(P~),psi(Q~)) is uniform on F_p^2 for every k (verified by exhaustive enumeration in the cases above; the argument is psi(P~+eps)=psi(P~)+(#E mod p) z(eps)/p). For lifts with Q~=[k]P~, psi(Q~)=k psi(P~) exactly.
