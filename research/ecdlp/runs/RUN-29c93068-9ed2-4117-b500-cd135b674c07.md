### T1 Structure-aware SAT (sat_ladder.py, sat_affine_cmp.py, analyze_t1.py, out_*.jsonl, log_*.txt)
Encoding E1: x-only Montgomery ladder (projective X:Z, constant x(P), a24; 10 mod-p multiplications per key bit, no inverses or exceptional cases), Montgomery curves with #E = 4r, r an n-bit prime, n = 5..8. Comparator: round-10 affine Weierstrass encoding, n = 5..10. Solvers: Glucose4, CaDiCaL, CryptoMiniSat with native XOR gates (E1x). 3 instances per n.
- The ladder encoding is much larger and much harder. At n=5 it has 22.9k-28.8k variables vs 4.5k-5.7k for affine, and Glucose4 did not solve ANY of the 7 ladder runs (n=5..7) within 120 s (about 0.5-0.8M conflicts). Affine n=5 takes 0.2-0.5 s. CaDiCaL on the ladder: n=5 solved in 10.6, 71, 125 s (affine n=5: 0.03-0.2 s); n=6 all 3 censored; n=7 1 of 3 solved (121 s). CryptoMiniSat+XOR on the ladder: n=5 solved in 55-73 s (re-validated at 82-94 s), n=6,7 censored.
- Fitted exponents (affine, n=5..10, censored counted at the cap, so they are lower bounds): Glucose4 conflicts r^1.27 [1.21, 1.33], seconds r^1.55; CaDiCaL conflicts r^1.47 [1.25, 1.73], seconds r^1.85. This reproduces round 10 (1.23). Ladder fits are not meaningful (18 of 25 runs censored, at most 2-3 sizes); the printed ladder exponents (e.g. Glucose -0.37, CMS 0.53) are artefacts of censoring at the cap and MUST NOT be read as sub-rho scaling.
- Native XOR (CryptoMiniSat) did not help: affine n=5..8 took 0.5-88 s (vs 0.03-25 s for CaDiCaL/Glucose), n=9 and 10 mostly censored.
- Cardinality: no natural cardinality constraint exists for a uniform key (only 1 <= k < r), so Minicard/Gluecard were not run (not applicable, not a negative test).
- Correctness: all solved Glucose/CaDiCaL keys verified by a Python ladder or scalar multiplication (0 wrong). The first CMS runs reported key_ok=false because of an off-by-one in model indexing (my bug; CMS models are 1-indexed). Fixed in the code, and re-run on 9 small instances (val_*.jsonl): all keys correct. The CMS rows in out_*cms* have invalid key_ok flags but unaffected timings; SAT status for those rows is unverified except in the re-run.
Verdict T1: NEGATIVE for the pre-registered rule. No encoding reached the sub-0.5 exponent; the x-only ladder is far worse than the affine circuit at equal n; XOR-native solving is slower. The round-10 observation stands: the cost is the solver inverting the modular multiplication circuits (a floor independent of key entropy), and the ladder adds multiplications. No pre-registered "promising" criterion is met.

### T2 Hensel-section cocycle (t2_cocycle.py, summarize_t2.py, out_t2.jsonl)
Prime-order curves, p = 1019, 4111, 12007, 30011 (two curves each, seeds 11, 12), random lift mod p^3.
Positive results (exact, all 8 curves): with l the Hensel set-theoretic lift (x in [0,p)), c(R) = l(R)+l(P)-l(R+P) in E_1, z = -X/Y additive, d1 = z(c)/p (the explicit cocycle eta) and d2 = z(c)/p^2 (the p^3 digit), g(R) = z([N] l(R))/p:
 1. g((k+1)P) = g(kP) + g(P) - N d1(kP), so g(kP) = k g(P) - N S_k, S_k = sum_{i<k} d1(iP), and N S_N = N g(P) (checked mod p^2; direct [N] multiplication matches).
 2. The round-10 section digit satisfies u2(R) = lambda y(R) g(R) with lambda = -2/N mod p (checked for all points / 120 sampled). So u2 and the "Birkhoff sum" S_k encode the same data. This is an exact restatement of the Smart-style error term (HYP-f85d20a1 context), not a new algorithm.
 3. g is odd: g(-R) = -g(R), hence S_{N-k} = g(P) - S_k.
Negative results: Berlekamp-Massey linear complexity equals the random value (n/2, window 2500) for d1, d2, S, g, u2, u3 on all curves; no P-recursive relation (order <= 3-4, degree <= 3-4); no polynomial of degree <= 12 in (x, y) represents d1, d2, g or S (positive control x^3+xy found, random not); no k-mod-m dependence (ANOVA, m in 2..7,8,16: no test below the Bonferroni threshold 0.001/42; the smallest analytic p ~ 0.0016 is expected among about 300 tests and did not replicate); positive controls (linear, k g^k) fire (BM 2), random and shuffled controls pass. u3 (digit 2 of the p^2-multiplier section) was computed directly for p <= 12007 and is also null.
Registered flag raised: the max-DFT spectral statistic of S and g gave |z| = 4-11 against a shuffle null in 9 (curve, sequence) cases. Post-hoc diagnosis (t2c, t2d; labelled post-hoc): g is odd, so the shuffle null is wrong; a null that respects g(N-k) = -g(k) (uniform odd g', S'_k = (k g(P) - g'_k)/N) gives z in [-2.1, 2.6] on all 5 dumped curves. The d1 increments themselves are uniform (std ratio about 1.0), with no lag-1/2 correlation and no spectrum. So the flag was a null-model artefact, not structure; this is a lesson for earlier spectral tests (check symmetries of the sequence).

### T2b / Idea 3: lift-variation of the section defect (t2b_liftvar.py, out_t2b.jsonl)
Required fields.
- Property exploited: the defect g_L depends on the chosen lift L=(A+p a', B+p b') of the curve; for Q = kP and each lift, k g_L(P) - N S_k^L = g_L(Q) with g_L(P), g_L(Q) computable in polynomial time. Many lifts give many equations; if the lift-dependence of S_k^L were algebraic or low-dimensional, the unknown count could stay bounded while equations grow.
- Barrier escaped: single-lift information-freeness (HYP-f85d20a1 concerns independent lifts of a point, not a family of lifts of the curve with the same exact section); not ruled out by earlier work.
- To prove: dependence of g_L on (a', b') is affine and the S^alpha, S^beta sums are algebraic coboundaries (so that k becomes determined).
- Kill: Delta_L polynomial-free, and the unknown count equals the equation count. Cheapest test: here (minutes).
- Result (p = 307, 307, 1019, 1019, 4111; 8 lifts each): g_L - g_0 is exactly affine in (a', b') (5/5 identities on all 5 curves), so the span of {1, k, g_L over 8 lifts} has rank 5 = 2 + 3, i.e. lifts add only 2 independent directions at first order. Counting: unknowns k, S^0, S^alpha, S^beta (4) versus equations from the constant, a' and b' coefficients (3). The system is under-determined by one, and every higher lift order adds as many unknowns as equations. Delta_a, Delta_b, Delta/y, Delta*y are not polynomials of degree <= 20 in (x, y); BM complexity full; k-mod-m ANOVA null (one uncorrected p = 0.005, not replicated). Verdict: KILLED at tested sizes; it reduces to "is the lift-derivative a coboundary", which the null polynomial fits and the open count say no to. (Higher precision p^4+ not tried.)
- Prior work: Smart (2000), Satoh-Araki, Semaev for N = p; canonical lift (Satoh) mentioned as unverified recollection; no source checked beyond snippets.
