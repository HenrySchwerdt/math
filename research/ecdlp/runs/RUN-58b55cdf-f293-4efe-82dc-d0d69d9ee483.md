## Headline
**NEGATIVE: no ambient group of type (a), (b), (c) escapes the supply bound, and (d) reduces to the same lemma (or to MOV/FR).** One universal lemma explains why: a computable push-down turns *every* relation in G into a relation on the cyclic group Z/r, so an ambient group can only (i) lower the supply by the factor |G|/r >= 1 and (ii) make the relation *finder* cheaper. It cannot add relations. Nothing here beats sqrt(r); no speedup is claimed. These are heuristic/exact-toy statements, not Lean-verified.

## 2. The universal lemma (derived; elementary)
Setting: ambient finite abelian group G, a computable homomorphism relationship with the target subgroup T = <P> of prime order r (|E(F_p)| = r), base B of s elements of G, relations Sum n_i b_i = 0 (length m).
- **L1 (push-down, any direction).** Let psi: G -> E(F_p) be a homomorphism (b: a cover/Albanese map; d: quotient to E of a generalized Jacobian), or let c = |G|/r^v with r-part of G cyclic of order r and use "multiply by the cofactor" G -> G_r = Z/r (a polynomial-time group-operation map, injective on G_r since gcd(c, r) = 1; HYP-caa15771/Transfer.lean T1 is the abstract fact that a hom of a prime-order group is zero or injective). Then every relation among b_i in G is a relation among psi(b_i) in Z/r with the same coefficients. So R_G(B) is a subset of R_{Z/r}(psi(B)).
- **L2 (supply, heuristic uniform model).** A random m-tuple of base elements closes with probability about 1/|G|, so supply = s^m/|G| (m = 2,3: matches the measured 2 s^3/(3p) of inventor E for G = E(F_p)). Since |G| >= r, supply_G <= supply_E for the same s. For G = E x A (any isogeny-type ambient), |G| = r |A|, so the loss is exactly |A(F_p)| ~ p^{g-1}.
- **L3 (accounting; script f3).** Need s^{m-1} >= |G| = p^g, so s = p^{g/(m-1)}; sparse linear algebra costs ~ m s^2, a generic list-merge relation finder ~ s^{ceil(m/2)}; total exponent (units log_p): max(2g/(m-1), g ceil(m/2)/(m-1)). Output of `f3_accounting.py` (exit 0): g=1: m=3 -> 1.0, m=4 -> 0.667, m=8 -> 0.571, m=59 -> 0.517 (monotone toward 1/2 from above: 1/2 + 1/(2(m-1)) for even m); g=2: >= 1.0175 for all m up to 59; g=3: >= 1.526; g=4: >= 2.035. So with a *generic* finder every G has exponent > 1/2 and growing with g: the ambient group only increases |G|. The generic-finder floor 1/2 is the black-box (Shoup-type) barrier (recollection). The algebraic finders (Cantor, Groebner, Weil restriction) lower the finder term only; they do not change s (unknowns) or s^2 (solving) and the exponent 2g/(m-1)... which is where each case below dies.
Caveat: L2 assumes the base behaves like random elements of G (inventor E measured this for G = E(F_p), 4 bases, within 2%); structured bases (the only found closure is the Z[w]-identity set, round 14) are not covered.

## 3. Candidate (a): E(F_p) in E(F_{p^n}), Gaudry/Diem descent over F_p
Formulas: group order N = |E(F_{p^n})| ~ p^n; Gaudry/Diem factor base V = {P : x(P) in F_p}; full-size |V| ~ p; relations by decomposing a generic R into n points of V (supply plentiful, finder cost poly via Groebner for fixed n); unknowns ~ p; cost (Diem, cited via HYP-ad5bc0e3) ~ p^{2-2/n} for the whole base, i.e. >= p^{1} for n >= 2... but for the F_p-subgroup the following is stronger.
Which points are in V (exact, and verified):
- n odd: y^2 = f(x) in F_p with y in F_{p^n} forces y in F_p (odd degree), so V = E(F_p) exactly.
- n even: V is inside E(F_p) union E^tw(F_p) (twist, embedded in F_{p^2}), and for odd r = |E(F_p)| prime the group generated is H = E(F_p) x E^tw(F_p) (the two meet only in E[2](F_p) = 0).
- **Consequence ("already in the factor base").** R in E(F_p) is itself a base element; Weil restriction of S_{n+1}(x_1..x_n, x_R) = 0 is *one* F_p-equation in n unknowns (x_R in F_p, so the n coordinate equations are dependent), with ~ p^{n-1} solutions that are all prime-field decompositions. For n = 2 and H a direct product, a closed relation needs both components to vanish: a mixed relation E + T... requires a single nonzero point to be 0, impossible; so closed triples are only EEE and TTT.
- **Exact check (f1, p = 1009, 2003, 4001, seed 1,2,3, s = 3 sqrt p, ordered pairs i<=j):** closed triples observed by type: p=1009: EEE 510, TTT 256, mixed 0; p=2003: EEE 692, TTT 448, mixed 0; p=4001: EEE 650, TTT 1034, mixed 0. Also #E(F_{p^2}) = #E #E^tw verified numerically (e.g. 3929 x 4075 = 16010675).
- Push-down = multiplication by the cofactor / projection onto the E-component; polynomial; injective on the r-part. Pure-E relations are exactly the prime-field relations, so (a) is a **special case of prime-field index calculus** with the same s^m/p supply. Nothing is added; the TTT relations are a wasted half of the base.
Total exponent: the prime-field exponent (inventor E: m = 2 ties rho at 1.22 sqrt p; ECDLP-12/16: m = 3 linear algebra already exceeds sqrt p). **Exact obstruction: V lies in a direct product whose twist factor decouples (n even) or equals E(F_p) (n odd).** Killed as a route to a new bound; not killed as "exponent >= 1/2 proved" (prime-field m >= 4 remains the open item of rounds 13-14; a G-based method cannot be better than it).
For a non-base-change E' over F_{p^n}: an F_p-point group does not embed into E'(F_{p^n}) except by a hom, and L1 applies (the transfer barrier, Transfer.lean T1-T2).

## 4. Candidate (b): J(C) for a cover pi: C -> E of degree e, genus g over F_p; base = degree-1 places
Formulas: |J(C)(F_p)| ~ p^g, r | |J| (E is a quotient by pi_*), place count ~ p; unknowns = s <= p (log of each place in J_r); relation supply for a random m-tuple of places = s^m/p^g/m!-ish by L2, and for m = g (reduced divisors) a random divisor is s-smooth with probability (s/p)^g/g!. Cost per relation (p/s)^g g! polylog. Total = s (p/s)^g = p^g s^{1-g}; with linear algebra s^2 the balance s = p^{g/(g+1)} gives exponent 2g/(g+1) (g=2: 4/3, the classical double-large-prime-free bound, recollection); with large primes GTTD gives 2-2/g (g=2: 1; g=3: 4/3) [cited via HYP-ad5bc0e3]. All >= 1.
Push-down: pi_*(Sum n_i P_i) = Sum n_i pi(P_i): m group additions in E; kernel of pi_* on J(F_p) is the complementary abelian variety A(F_p); injective on the r-part iff r does not divide |A(F_p)| (checkable by point counting; otherwise J_r has rank 2, which only enlarges the unknown space).

## 5. Candidate (c): E x E' glued along E[N] into a genus-2 Jacobian (Frey-Kani)
Which level N, over which field (derived, with the Kani/Frey statements as **recollection, not retrieved**): the gluing needs a Frobenius-equivariant anti-isometry psi: E[N] -> E'[N]; for N = 2 that is an F_p[Frob]-isomorphism of F_2^2 (every such iso preserves the unique alternating form, det = 1 in F_2). For prime-order r (odd) E has no F_p-rational 2-torsion, so Frobenius acts on E[2]\{0} without fixed points, i.e. as a 3-cycle (order 3 in GL_2(F_2)); any E'/F_p whose cubic is irreducible has the same module, so **N = 2 works over F_p itself** for every E' with irreducible 2-division cubic and j(E') != j(E) (geometric product case excluded by Kani; recollection). The bielliptic example in section 4 is precisely an N = 2 gluing (E'' = complement; the cover has degree 2). N = 3, 4, 5: the twisted modular curves X_E^-(N) have genus 0, so F_p-points exist (conic over a finite field; recollection about genus). The glued curve has genus 2 for every N; N only sets the degrees of the maps C -> E, E'.
Formulas: unknowns s, N-group |J| = r |E'| ~ p^2, supply s^m/p^2, push-down = pi_{E*}, cost O(m) additions, injective on the r-part iff r does not divide |E'(F_p)| N (E' can be chosen coprime). Exponent: same as (b), g = 2: >= 1. Choosing |E'(F_p)| = r too makes the r-part rank 2, which only doubles the unknown space.
**Exact obstruction:** J ~ E x E' and the relation condition is componentwise (product obstruction of (a), verified in f1/f2), so (c) is (b) with g = 2. Killed.

## 6. Candidate (d): other ambient groups
(d1) Generalized Jacobian / ray class group J_m (extension of E by a torus or G_a, modulus m). |J_m(F_p)| = r (p-1)^k or r p^k; relations need f = 1 mod m, an extra congruence; the r-part is canonically that of E (r does not divide p-1 or p for r prime near p), so L1 applies with psi = quotient to E (computable: forget the local data). Supply is lower by |torus(F_p)| ~ p per modulus point. The torus factor has L(1/2)/L(1/3)-type index calculus but it is the *kernel*, which is irrelevant to the r-part. Killed (arithmetic, recollection for the extension structure).
(d2) Multiplicative group of F_{p^k}: this is the dual direction and the only ambient group with sub-exponential index calculus. Pairing (MOV/FR) is a polynomial-time map E[r] -> mu_r in F_{p^k}^*, injective (nondegenerate) on the r-part; but by Transfer.lean T2 (r | |target|, i.e. r | p^k - 1) it exists nonzero only for embedding degree k, and HYP-eff2cfbb bounds the number of traces with k <= M by M(M+1) per q, i.e. a negligible fraction of prime-order curves; generic k ~ r. Exact obstruction: arithmetic divisibility, not supply. Killed on generic instances (known, HYP-caa15771).
(d3) Any other abelian variety A with computable hom to E(F_p): L1/L2 apply verbatim; |A| >= r so supply cannot increase. The general theorem for random-like bases: **no ambient group can increase supply; |G|/r is a pure loss.**

## 7. What this does not rule out (stated plainly)
1. Structured (non-random-like) bases adapted to the extra structure of G: the supply heuristic L2 does not apply; round 14 found only trivial Z[w]-identities, nothing in G tested here.
2. Prime-field relations of length m >= 4 with a finder cheaper than s^{ceil(m/2)} (the finder term in L3 is generic). L3 shows even g = 1 only reaches exponent 1/2 + 1/(2(m-1)) with generic merging, so a *new* finder is required; no ambient group provides one.
3. Heuristic uniformity of G-relation supply was verified only through f1/f2 at p <= 8009 (f2 weakly); no formal statement proved; none Lean-checked; no independent review.

## 8. Answer to the framing question
The "supply/unknown ratio" cannot be improved by embedding because L1 makes the ambient relation set a subset of the Z/r relation set: ratio(G) <= ratio(E) * (r/|G|). What embeddings change is *finder cost*, and Gaudry/Diem-type descents buy a cheap finder only by taking the whole base (s ~ p, exponent >= 1), while (a) additionally degenerates to the prime field because E(F_p) is already inside the base.
