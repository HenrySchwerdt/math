## Exact bound statements
(A) Merai, Lemma 1 (as printed; his attribution is to Chen-Li-Xiao [4]). Q in E(F_p) of order N, chi nonprincipal, f in F_p(E) with f != z^d for all z in F_p(E) (d = order of chi; his remark: it suffices to check l = d, cf. Perel'muter 1963):
 |sum_{x=0}^{t-1} chi(f((a+bx)Q))| < |Supp(f)| p^{1/2} (1 + log N), for 1 <= a <= a+(t-1)b <= N.
 Caveat: he defines Supp(f) as zeros/poles in E(F_p). Read literally this is wrong (e.g. g = y has no F_p-rational zeros when r is odd, giving bound 0); the correct quantity is geometric (see C). I treat "|Supp(f)|" as the number of geometric points of the divisor, which is what the proof route (Weil) needs. Unverified what [4] says.
(B) Kohel-Shparlinski Thm 1 (additive version): omega any group character of E(F_q), psi nontrivial additive character, f nonconstant with polar divisor sum n_i P_i: |S(omega,psi,f)| <= sum m_i deg(P_i) q^{1/2}, m_i <= n_i+1 with equality iff gcd(n_i, q) = 1; hence |S| <= 2 deg(f) q^{1/2}, and (1+deg f) q^{1/2} if the poles are supported at one place; Cor. 1: same bound 2 deg(f) q^{1/2} for sums over a subgroup H. Incomplete sums: Lemma 2 (sum over a of e_n(ak) bounded by n(1 + log n)) gives extra factor (1 + log). Hypothesis: psi nontrivial, f nonconstant.
(C) Multiplicative version, derived here from K-S Props. 1-2 (Weil/Bombieri) and tame Kummer ramification (my derivation, not a quotation). Let m = ord(chi), chi~ = chi o g the Kummer character of F_p(E)(g^{1/m}); omega pulled back from the Lang isogeny is unramified. chi~ is ramified exactly at the geometric places P with m not dividing ord_P(g), tamely (m | p-1), conductor exponent 1. Put D = sum over such places of deg P. Then (g(E)=1) L(t, omega chi~) is a polynomial of degree D with all roots of modulus sqrt(p) provided omega chi~ is nontrivial, and
 sum_{Q in E(F_p)} (omega chi~)(Q) = -(theta_1+...+theta_D), |theta_i| = sqrt(p),
where the places of degree 1 not ramified (zeros/poles of g of order divisible by m, e.g. O for g = x, chi quadratic) contribute their local value omega(Q) chi(leading coefficient) instead of 0. Therefore
 | S(omega, chi, g) + eps | <= D sqrt(p), eps = sum of the unramified-support local terms (|eps| <= number of F_p-points in the unramified support).
Examples (chi quadratic): g = x, x+1, 2x: D = 2 (two zeros, pole at O of even order -> unramified, eps = chi(lc) = 1, 1, chi(2)); g = x^2+x+1: D = 4; g = y: D = 4 (zeros at the 2-torsion points, one place of degree 3 since r is odd, plus a tamely ramified pole at O); g = xy: D = 6. Hypothesis: g not an m-th power times constant over F-bar_p (otherwise omega chi~ trivial for the matching omega). Constant "2 deg(g)": D <= (number of geometric zeros+poles) <= 2 deg(g), so |S| <= 2 deg(g) sqrt(p) + O(deg g) is a safe uniform statement; D itself is smaller (e.g. 2 = deg g for g = x since the pole is unramified).
 Numerical check of (C): max over all c != 0, 80 curves, p < 3000: |F+eps|/sqrt(p) = 1.999 (x), 1.998 (x+1), 1.999 (2x) [bound 2; with eps = chi(2) needed for 2x], 3.628 (x^2+x+1) [bound 4], 2.000 (y) [bound 4], 4.354 (xy) [bound 6]. `experiments/round3-nt/out_weil_check.txt`. No violation; the D = 2 bounds are essentially attained (sharp up to o(1)).

## Derivations for full- and half-range sums
Full range. r prime => E(F_p) = <P> cyclic, and every character of E(F_p) is psi_c(kP) = e(ck/r) (c mod r). Hence F(c) = S(psi_c, chi, g) exactly, for all c != 0 (and c = 0 is the trivial group character, covered by (C) because chi~ is nontrivial). By (C): |F(c)| <= D sqrt(p) + |eps| for every c, so the normalised X_c = F(c)/sqrt(N) satisfies |X_c| <= D sqrt(p/N) + o(1) = D (1 + O(p^{-1/2})) since N = r - O(1) and |r - p - 1| <= 2 sqrt p. So max_c periodogram <= D^2 (1+o(1)) (4 for g = x), versus ~ log r (about 7-8) for a random sequence: this is a deterministic, bounded-tail statement, but only D-dependent, not a variance statement.
Half range / incomplete. f_k = f(kP) is r-periodic. For real theta, with F^(a) = sum_{k mod r} f_k e(-ak/r), f_k = (1/r) sum_a F^(a) e(ak/r) and
 sum_{k=1}^h f_k e(k theta) = (1/r) sum_{a mod r} F^(a) sum_{k=1}^h e(k(theta + a/r)).
|sum_{k=1}^h e(k phi)| <= min(h, 1/(2||phi||)) and sum_a min(h, 1/(2||theta + a/r||)) <= h + r sum_{j=1}^{r/2} 1/j <= r(1/2 + log r). With |F^(a)| <= B := D sqrt(p) + |eps| this gives |sum_{k=1}^h f_k e(k theta)| <= B (1/2 + 1 + log r) = O(D sqrt(p) log p), for EVERY theta (in particular theta = c/h as in the observation). Normalised by sqrt(h): |X|^2/h <= O(D^2 log^2 p). This is Merai's/K-S's (1 + log N) factor (K-S Lemma 2 / Lemma 3). The log^2 is not removable by this method and makes the bound vacuous for variance: half-range completion gives only O(log^2 p), whereas the observed anomaly is an O(1) effect. The half-range sum is NOT a group-character sum, so no exact analogue of F(c) exists.
Note (even sequences). For g = x we have f(-Q) = f(Q), so F(c) is REAL for all c and the full-range periodogram is |F|^2/N = (real variable)^2 -- a real Gaussian baseline has variance 2, not 1. The half-range complex DFT of an iid sequence has variance 1.

## Distribution/moment predictions
Exact identities (proved; Parseval and orthogonality on the cyclic group G, valid for any f):
(P2) sum_{c mod r} |F(c)|^2 = r sum_k |f_k|^2 = r N. Hence the mean of I(c) := |F(c)|^2/N over c mod r is exactly 1; over c != 0 it is 1 + O(D^2/r) because I(0) <= D^2(1+o(1)). The mean is therefore ~1 regardless of anything: flatness can only be a statement about higher moments.
(P4) |F|^4 = sum f(k1)f(k2) conj f(k3) conj f(k4) e(c(k1+k2-k3-k4)/r); summing over c gives r times the indicator of Q1+Q2 = Q3+Q4. So
 sum_c |F(c)|^4 = r sum_R |A(R)|^2, A(R) = sum_{Q1+Q2=R} f(Q1) f(Q2) (cyclic autocorrelation f*f),
 M4 := (1/r) sum_c I(c)^2 = (1/N^2) sum_R |A(R)|^2 = #-weighted solutions of Q1+Q2=Q3+Q4 with weight chi(g(Q1)g(Q2)g(Q3)g(Q4))/N^2 (for real chi).
Split R = O: A(O) = sum_Q chi(g(Q)g(-Q)) = +-N exactly iff g(-Q)/g(Q) is a square times +-1 (g "symmetric": x, x+1, y, x^2+x+1, xy, but not x+y, y+1), else |A(O)| = O(D' sqrt p). Hence
 M4 = delta_sym + (p/N) E_{R != O}[ |A(R)|^2 / p ] + O(1/r), delta_sym in {0,1}.
For R != O, A(R) = sum_Q chi(h_R(Q)), h_R = g(Q) g(R-Q), is a mixed sum of the same type (trivial omega; Weil => |A(R)| <= D_R sqrt p), so M4 is controlled by the second moment over R of Weil numbers.
Random-model baselines (heuristic, not theorems): independent +-1 values: if f is not symmetric, E|A(R)|^2 = N, M4 = 2 (complex Gaussian, periodogram Exp(1), var(I) = 1). If f is symmetric then A(R) = 2 (sum over unordered pairs {Q, R-Q}) so E A^2 = 2N and M4 = 1 + 2 = 3 (real Gaussian, var(I) = 2). Parity(x) and hash bits (x-symmetric but not Legendre) give exactly this (M4 = 2.98, 3.0).
Heuristic prediction for Weil-structured f (conjecture, supported numerically; no proof, no source found): F(c)/sqrt(N) = -(sum of D unit Weil numbers) behaves like the trace of a random element of a compact monodromy group K_D: for the symmetric case F is real, so K_D is symplectic or orthogonal; the explicit candidates are
 g = x (D = 2, K = USp(2) = SU(2)): u = 2cos(phi), phi Sato-Tate, E u^2 = 1, E u^4 = 2, E u^6 = 5 (Catalan), M4 = 2, var(I) = 1, |u| <= 2;
 symmetric g with D = 4 (x^2+x+1): K = USp(4): E u^2 = 1, E u^4 = 3, E u^6 = 14, M4 = 3, var(I) = 2, |u| <= 4 (a real Gaussian would have E u^6 = 15);
 non-symmetric g (x+y, y+1): F complex, K = U(D)-like, M4 = 2 as for Exp(1).
Mechanism for why g = x differs from x^2+x+1 (derived, heuristic on the monodromy step): A(R) is a sum over the quotient P^1 = E/tau_R, tau_R(Q) = R-Q, of (1+eta(u)) chi(t(u)), with t = x(Q)x(R-Q) of degree 2 on P^1 (two simple zeros, one double pole). The first piece sum_u chi(t(u)) is a quadratic character sum of a degree-2 function on P^1 with only two ramified points: L-degree 2g-2+2 = 0, i.e. O(1), no Weil number. Only the eta-twisted piece carries Weil numbers (one irreducible block, E|A|^2/p ~ 1) so M4 = 1 + 1 = 2. For g = x^2+x+1 the first piece has 4 ramified points (rank 2) in addition to the eta-twisted piece: two independent blocks, E|A|^2/p ~ 2, M4 = 3. This explains the dichotomy {x, x+1, 2x} vs {x^2+x+1, x(x+1), x^2+1, xy}. For g = y the same count predicts two blocks, yet the data give one (M4 = 2.00; semicircle); this is UNEXPLAINED (open).
Half-range variance prediction (derived under a model, then checked). Write X(theta) = C + iS with C = sum_{k<=h} f_k cos(k theta), S the sine sum, theta = 2 pi c/r. For symmetric f, C = F(c)/2 exactly, and by the completion formula S is a Hilbert-type weighted sum S = sqrt(h/2) (2/pi) sum_{j odd} u_{c+j}/j (u_a := F(a)/sqrt N; parity restriction from D_h(m) being ~ -1/2 for even m and r/(i pi m) for odd m). Model: u_a independent copies of a law nu with E u^2 = 1, excess kurtosis kappa = E u^4 - 3. Then Var(u^2) = 2 + kappa; since sum_j v_j^4 = 1/3 for v_j = (2/pi)/j (j odd), Var(S_n^2) = 2 + kappa/3, and
 Var(|X|^2/h) = (Var(u^2) + Var(S_n^2))/4 = 1 + kappa/3.
 Gaussian (kappa = 0): 1. Semicircle (kappa = -1): 2/3 = 0.667. This reproduces the stated observation (0.66) and the controls (1.0). Numerics: x, y, x+1, 2x give 0.659-0.681 (above); symmetric g with D = 4, parity, hash bits give 0.97-1.03; non-symmetric x+y, y+1 give 0.98-1.02 (these have complex F, outside the model, behave like Exp(1)).
So: the observed 0.66 = 1 + kappa/3 with kappa = -1 is explained quantitatively by "F(c)/sqrt N is semicircle (SU(2) Sato-Tate) distributed", which in turn follows (heuristically) from Weil's D = 2 plus a rank-2 symplectic monodromy. It requires NO leakage assumption. The statement "variance < 1 because of Weil" is correct only in this precise sense: kappa < 0 because bounded support |u| <= D forces E u^4 < 3 whenever D is small; for D >= 4 the support bound is not binding and kappa ~ 0 (USp(4) has kappa = 0 in the 4th moment).

## Relevance to discrete log
None positive. All quantities above are properties of the sequence {chi(g(kP))}_k as a whole (equidistribution in the group G), computed from the public data (E, P) without k. Spectral flatness/semicircle statistics concern the multiset of Fourier coefficients F(c) and are invariants of the curve; they do not depend on a secret exponent. Weil's bound is anti-leak: |F(c)|/N <= D/sqrt(p)+o(1) means every Fourier correlation between f(kP) and a linear phase e(ck/r) in k is at most about D p^{-1/2}.
A leak would be a statement of this type: there are (poly-time computable) maps Phi from (E, P, Q = [k]P) to a bit/short string and a nonempty family of publicly known sets A != B of exponents (residue classes, short intervals, bit patterns of k) such that for a random k with [k]P = Q, |Pr[Phi(E,P,Q) = 1 | k in A] - Pr[Phi = 1 | k in B]| >= 1/poly(log p) for infinitely many (E, P); equivalently a non-negligible advantage of a polynomial-time predictor of some function of k from Q. Under this criterion the spectral anomaly gives no advantage: the periodogram of the full sequence is independent of any particular Q. A genuine spectral leak would require a short-interval or correlation statistic beating the completion bound D(1+log r)/sqrt(p) (e.g. a correlation c0 > D log p/sqrt p between f(kP) and some computable k-dependent bit), which is not suggested by any observation here (observed maxima of the full-range statistic are at the Weil bound for D = 2).

## Limitations

- Toy curves only; p < 3000; seeds limited; moments estimated from about 10^5 values per function; 4-digit agreement in M4 does not exclude O(p^{-1/2}) corrections.
