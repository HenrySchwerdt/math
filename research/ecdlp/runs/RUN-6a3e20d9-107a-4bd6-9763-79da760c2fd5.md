## 1. Inputs

- HYP-caa15771 (Lean-verified). A homomorphism on a prime-order subgroup is injective or trivial, and nontrivial only if r divides the order of the target. Guardrail used: a transfer E(F_p) -> J_C(F_p) must be injective (needs r not dividing the degree d). Then r divides #J_C(F_p) automatically.
- Setting: E/F_p, p prime, r = #E prime. Cover pi: C -> E over F_p of degree d and genus g >= 2. Genus 1 means an isogeny, so it is excluded. pi^* on Pic^0 is explicit, since pulling back a point means taking the fibre ideal, with no need to find the fibre points. pi_* pi^* = [d], so pi^* is injective on E(F_p)[r] when r does not divide d.
- Structure: J_C ~ E x A with dim A = g-1 (Poincare). #J_C(F_p) is about p^g. The target subgroup S = pi^*(E(F_p)) has order r, about p. Write L = log q (natural log) with q = p.

## 3. Regime analysis

Notation: q = p, L = ln q, #J_C(F_q) = q^{g(1+o(1))}. The target lives in S of order r, about q. Rho in S costs sqrt(r), about q^{1/2}, whatever the ambient group. The question is whether an index calculus bound on J_C is below q^{1/2}.

### 3.1 Proposition A (proved here from S2, S3, S5; theorem-level hypotheses)

(i) For each fixed g>=2 the cited bound O~(q^{2-2/g}) has exponent e(g) = 2-2/g >= 1.
(ii) For each fixed d>=4 the plane-curve bound O~(q^{2-2/(d-2)}) has exponent >= 1.
(iii) The O~(q^2) bound of S1 has exponent 2 (S1 is unverified).
In all three cases the exponent is >= 1 > 1/2. So for every fixed g, d the cited upper bound exceeds sqrt(q) for q large enough, that is, for all q beyond a threshold depending on the constants hidden in O~ and on g.

Derivation: the arithmetic of exponents alone. Remarks:
- The smallest case is g=2 (and the plane quartic, g=3), with exponent exactly 1. This is the same as rho on the genus 2 group of size q^2, so index calculus ties with rho there, and rho on E in S costs q^{1/2}.
- This compares an upper bound with sqrt(q). It does not prove a lower bound for the problem.
- The theorems are for fixed g. They give nothing for g growing with q, and the O~ constants may grow fast with g.
- S3 and S2 need a cyclic (or known-structure) class group. J_C(F_p) is isogenous to E x A and is not cyclic in general. The structure must be computable, which is feasible for g=2 and costly for larger g.
- d=3 is not covered by S5 (d>=4), and plugging d=3 into the formula would give exponent 0, which is meaningless.

Status: **proved here from cited statements** (arithmetic on verified hypotheses). The same holds for the S1 clause, with S1 unverified.

### 3.2 Proposition B (proved here from S6/S7, conditional on the regimes stated there)

Let L = ln q and N = q^g, so ln N = gL. Write L_N(alpha, c) = exp(c (ln N)^alpha (ln ln N)^{1-alpha}). Compare with sqrt(q) = exp(L/2).

(a) L(1/2) regime. Suppose g = lambda L with lambda >= delta > 0 (regime of S6 as recalled). Then L_N(1/2, c) = exp(c L sqrt(lambda (ln lambda + 2 ln L)(1+o(1)))). This is below exp(L/2) iff lambda(ln lambda + 2 ln L) < 1/(4c^2) (to leading order). For lambda >= delta the left side tends to infinity with L, so the inequality fails for all L > exp(1/(8 c^2 delta))/sqrt(delta) or so (up to lower order terms). Hence for any constants c, delta > 0, for q large enough the L_{q^g}(1/2, c) cost exceeds sqrt(q) = q^{1/2} for every g >= delta log q.
The bound is q^{omega(1)}, superpolynomial in q, for g >= delta L. (For g/L -> infinity the margin only grows.)

(b) L(1/3) regime. Suppose g >= L^2 (S7). Then (gL)^{1/3} >= L, so L_N(1/3, c) >= exp(c L (3 ln L)^{2/3}(1+o(1))). The ratio of the exponent to L/2 is at least 2c (3 ln L)^{2/3} -> infinity. So for any fixed c > 0 the cost exceeds sqrt(q) once L > exp((1/3)(1/(2c))^{3/2}). (If the L(1/3) constant (64k/9)^{1/3} of S7 were tiny, the threshold is large. For c >= 1 it is small.)

Status: **proved here from cited statements**, conditional on (a) the regime of S6 being g >= delta log q (unverified recollection) and (b) the heuristic status of S7. Not valid as a statement about the best possible algorithm.

### 3.3 The regime where the L-formula would be below sqrt(q), and why it is not a theorem

The inequality g ln(gL) < L/(4c^2) forces g = O(L/ln L). This is exactly the region outside S6 (g >= delta L) and S7 (g >= L^2). Where g = L^theta with theta < 1 the formula L_N(1/2, c) = exp(O(L^{(1+theta)/2} sqrt(ln L))) is below exp(L/2). But there is no theorem there. S7's own introduction states that for g fixed and q -> infinity the algorithms are exponential in log q. The statement S7b, "log q bounded by some polynomial in g", read literally with log q = g^k and k > 1, would lead to exp(c g^{(k+1)/2}), below sqrt(q) = exp(g^k/2). I flag this as an apparent conflict between S7b as summarised and the heuristic counting bound below. I resolve it by treating S7b as loose: the primary regimes are S6/S7.

### 3.4 Proposition C (heuristic, derived here): generic index calculus on J_C costs at least about q/20

Model: factor base F is a set of degree-1 places (|F| = x <= q + 2g sqrt(q) + 1, which is about q). A relation is obtained from a class in J whose divisor is a sum of at most g elements of F union -F. Relations are generic in the sense that a random element of S has the same smoothness density as a random element of J (this is the genericity heuristic, and is the point where a cover could break the argument).
- The number of such sums is at most (2x)^g / g!. #J >= (sqrt(q)-1)^{2g}. So the probability that a random class is F-smooth is at most (2x)^g / (g! #J), and the expected number of trials per relation is at least g! #J / (2x)^g.
- At least x relations are needed (one per unknown log).
- Total cost >= x g! (sqrt(q)-1)^{2g} / (2x)^g. For x = eta q with 0 < eta <= 1+o(1), this is >= eta q (g!/2^g) (1-q^{-1/2})^{2g} / eta^g. It is decreasing in eta for g >= 1, so the minimum is at eta about 1.
- For 1 <= g <= sqrt(q) we have g!/2^g >= 1/2 and (1-q^{-1/2})^{2g} >= e^{-2.1}, hence cost >= q/20 (rough constant) > sqrt(q) once q > 400.
Factor bases containing places of degree m >= 2 have |F| about q^m/m >= q, so at least |F| relations again costs >= q (this is the scale in S6/S7 where m = 1 is not enough). A factor base consisting only of degree >= 2 places is not analysed here.
Status: **heuristic** (modelled; not a theorem).

### 3.5 Why the extension-field successes do not transfer (derivation)

GHS/Gaudry/Diem (S4, S8, S9) work for E/F_{q^n}, n >= 2. The point is not the genus but that Weil restriction replaces the base field F_{q^n} by the smaller F_q, so the index calculus exponent is in q = p^{1/n}. For n = 3: q^{2-2/n}... in terms of Q = q^n the cost O~(Q^{4/9}), below Q^{1/2} (unverified recollection of the exponent; S8 verifies q^{10/7} for GF(q^3) in the ePrint abstract, which is Q^{10/21} < Q^{1/2}). For prime p, F_p has no proper subfield: Res_{F_p/F_p} E = E. Any cover C -> E over F_p gives J_C over the same field F_p, and Proposition A applies with q = p. Passing to E(F_{p^n}) and descending only gives Res E = E^n, a product that contains no new structure (the DL in E(F_p) embeds in it and decomposition there reduces to summation polynomials over E(F_p) with factor base about p).

## 4. Missing ingredient and the pulled-back relations

### 4.1 Lemma D (proved here; elementary)

Let pi: C -> E be any finite cover over F_p. For a point P in E(F_p), the pull-back relation is: the class of pi^*(P) - pi^*(O) in J_C(F_p) equals the sum of the places in the fibre. Fibres over distinct P are disjoint. So the relation matrix, with unknowns the logs of the places of C in the factor base, has disjoint row supports. Each unknown appears in at most one relation. Hence these relations determine no individual factor base log (unless d = 1). They only record that pi^* is a homomorphism. Their number is at most #E(F_p) = r, while the unknowns are about #C(F_p) = p(1+o(1)). Other relations (principal divisors on C unrelated to pi) are needed, and those are the generic ones of Proposition C.

### 4.2 Precise missing ingredient

A cover-based transfer beats sqrt(p) only if one can exhibit, for E/F_p prime field:
(I) a family of covers C -> E of degree prime to r, genus g(p), with J_C(F_p) group order and structure computable in time at most p^{1/2},
(II) a factor base F of size at most p^{1/2-eps}, *adapted to S*, so that the m-fold sums of F hit S with density sufficient to give at least |F| relations: heuristically |F|^m >= p^g (since S has density p^{1-g} in J_C), so m >= 2g summands for |F| <= p^{1/2}, and
(III) a decomposition algorithm for the m-fold sums in time p^{1/2-eps} per relation.
Taking F inside S gives BSGS (m = 2, |F| = sqrt(r), cost sqrt(r)), the generic bound. So all the gain would have to come from a non-generic decomposition in (III) using the geometry of C, and for g >= 2 the generic counts in Proposition C say that the geometry has no help unless the relations come from a structure other than the cover.

### 4.3 Covers E x_{P^1} P^1 via x -> h(t): genus by Riemann-Hurwitz (derived)

C = normalisation of E x_{P^1} P^1 (x: E -> P^1 degree 2, h: P^1 -> P^1 degree d), pi: C -> E has degree d. 2g-2 = deg R_pi. For generic h (2d-2 simple branch values, none a branch value of x) each branch value gives 2 points of E, each simply ramified: deg R_pi = 2(2d-2), so g = 2d-1. For h = t^d with E: y^2 = f(x), f a squarefree cubic, f(0) != 0, the cover is C_d: y^2 = f(t^d). Ramification: over the two points of E above x=0, pi is totally ramified (2(d-1)); over O, x has e=2 and h has e=d: for d odd one point, e=d (d-1); for d even two points e=d/2 (d-2). So 2g-2 = 3d-3 (d odd) or 3d-4 (d even), i.e. g = (3d-1)/2 for d odd and g = (3d-2)/2 for d even. d=2 gives g=2, the bielliptic case, with J_C ~ E x E_2, E_2: v^2 = u f(u). The smallest genus of a non-isogeny cover is 2. In every case g grows linearly in d, so large degree gives large genus, but r must not divide d. No case reaches g = O(log p) with a smooth-factor-base structure: this is Proposition C and Lemma D, not the genus, that is the obstruction.

## 5. Proposals (six fields each)

**P1. Cover-assisted index calculus using the fibre structure of pi.** (speculative)
- Property relied on: pi^* is an explicit homomorphism from E(F_p), and fibres of pi over rational points split completely with probability about 1/d (Kummer-type cover) or 1/d! (generic).
- Why it could help: pulled-back classes are automatically smooth when the fibre splits, so a fraction 1/d of E(F_p) gives "free" relations with no smoothness search.
- Must be proved: that free relations plus a modest number of generic relations determine the logs of F, with total cost < sqrt(p).
- What could invalidate it: Lemma D. Free relations have disjoint supports and carry no information beyond the homomorphism property. This invalidates the "free relations suffice" version outright. Only the "plus generic relations" version remains, which is Proposition C.
- Cheapest decisive test: T1 numerics, and T2.
- Relevant existing results: S2, S3, S5 (exponent >= 1); HYP-caa15771 (injectivity guardrail).

**P2. Large-genus Enge-Gaudry/ADH index calculus on J_C with g about c log p.** (speculative)
- Property relied on: L_{q^g}(1/2) subexponential complexity in the size of J_C.
- Why it could help: subexponential in gL means asymptotically below any exponential in gL, and for g = lambda L the target group is only a sqrt-sized piece.
- Must be proved: that the cost, expressed in L = ln p, is below L/2, which needs lambda(ln lambda + 2 ln L) < 1/(4c^2).
- What could invalidate it: Proposition B(a): for g >= delta L this fails for all large q. Proposition B(b) for g >= L^2.
- Cheapest decisive test: evaluate the exponent ratio (done, table in section 6: c=1, ratios from about 1.7 up to over 100 for the L(1/2) formula; the L(1/3) ratio is below 1 only at g = L/8, which is outside its validity).
- Relevant existing results: S6, S7.

**P3. Weil-descent-style (GHS) covers for prime-field E.** (speculative)
- Property relied on: GHS/Frey descent produces covers of E over a smaller field.
- Why it could help: reduces the base field, giving a q^{2-2/g} cost in a much smaller q.
- Must be proved: existence of a subfield, which a prime field lacks.
- What could invalidate it: F_p has no proper subfield, so Res_{F_p/F_p} E = E. No field-size reduction exists.
- Cheapest decisive test: none needed (the statement is a tautology about prime fields), but one can check that the Weil restriction of E_{F_{p^n}} to F_p is isogenous to E^n.
- Relevant existing results: S4, S8, S9.

**P4. Structure-adapted factor base (item II of 4.2).** (open; the only route left)
- Property relied on: a geometric description of S inside J_C allowing a factor base of size at most p^{1/2-eps} with decomposable m-fold sums, m >= 2g.
- Why it could help: if decomposition of a class in S into m elements of F could be done faster than |F|, it would beat generic.
- Must be proved: an efficient decomposition algorithm and density.
- What could invalidate it: F inside S is just BSGS. F outside S has density p^{1-g}, giving cost at least p^{g-1}/... per relation.
- Cheapest decisive test: for g=2, E x E_2, test whether summation-polynomial-style systems on C give decompositions of elements of S with F = places of degree 1 of small x-coordinate. Cost estimate by counting alone says no.
- Relevant existing results: Proposition C; HYP-caa15771.

## 7. Verdict

- For every fixed g, d the cited upper bounds on J_C(F_p) have exponent at least 1, so they do not beat sqrt(p) (Proposition A, proved from cited statements).
- In the large-genus regimes with proven or heuristic theorems (g >= delta log q for L(1/2); g >= (log q)^2 for L(1/3)) the stated cost is q^{omega(1)} (Proposition B).
- The region below sqrt(p) in the L-formulas (g = O(log q / log log q)) has no theorem, and a counting argument (Proposition C, heuristic) puts generic index calculus there at least about q/20.
- Pulled-back relations carry no extra information (Lemma D, with numerics), and prime fields have no Weil descent.
- So no regime of g beats sqrt(p) with known index calculus on a cover. What would be needed is item II-III of section 4.2, a structure-adapted factor base with fast decomposition inside S, for which no existing result or even a plausible mechanism was found.
- Risk: the single point where the conclusion depends on an unverified item is B(a), via the regime of S6 (g >= delta log q). The conflicting literal reading of S7b (3.3) is resolved only by the counting bound C.

## 8. Limitations

- The O~ constants in S2-S5 depend on g and are not stated in the sources I retrieved. Proposition A is therefore asymptotic in q for fixed g.
- The full texts of S1 and S6 were not accessible. Their regimes are recollections, flagged above.
- Proposition C is a heuristic counting model for degree-1 factor bases, not a lower bound for the problem. Generic algorithms (rho) in J_C also cost about sqrt(r).
- Section 4.3 treats only Kummer-type h(t) = t^d and generic h; non-Galois covers with special branching were not enumerated. Cover degree d must be prime to r.
- T2 was not run. No claim of formal verification. Status: derivations and heuristic, not a Lean-verified result.
