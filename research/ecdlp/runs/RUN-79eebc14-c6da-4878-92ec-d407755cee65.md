### The eight ideas (mechanism, gate, wall, result)

**C1. Whole-group Hankel/Toeplitz structure: linear complexity of the sequence x(kP).**
Mechanism: treat k -> x(kP) (and y) as a length-r periodic sequence over F_p and look for a short linear recurrence with Berlekamp-Massey (equivalently a low-rank Hankel matrix H_ij = x((i+j)P)). A recurrence of order L would put the sequence in a sum of L exponentials in k, giving structured linear algebra (Toeplitz solve, FFT in the exponent) over the whole group.
Gate: the Fourier expansion of an r-periodic sequence lives over F_{p^m}, m = ord_r(p) (the embedding degree); LC equals the number of non-zero Fourier coefficients, and Frobenius permutes them in orbits of size m. A small LC would force the eigenvalues zeta_r^t in F_{p^m}^*, and inverting a recurrence of that type is a discrete logarithm in F_{p^m}^*, which is exactly the MOV transfer (W-XFER); for large m there is nothing to gain even if LC were small. Not excluded by W-INT (those concern polynomials in x, not recurrences in k). Survives the paper gate only as a test.
Test (E1, 9 curves, bits 10/12/14, seeds 1-3): LC(x) = LC(y) = r exactly on all 9 curves, equal to the random r-periodic control (also r); planted order r/4 recurrence recovered exactly (LC = r/4 on all 9). Pre-registered kill rule (LC <= 0.9 r on some curve) not met. **Killed by test, wall W-XFER/Fourier density.** Prior work: linear complexity of elliptic-curve sequences has been studied (Hess-Shparlinski, recollection, unverified).

**C2. Pollard-Strassen compressibility of the baby-step object: is x(kP), y(kP) or P_m(X0) = prod_{j<=m}(X0 - x(jP)) P-recursive in the index?**
Mechanism: BSGS-style products over an arithmetic progression prod_{j<=m} f(j) cost O~(sqrt m) (not m) whenever the step j -> j+1 is a matrix with entries polynomial in j (Pollard-Strassen; Bostan-Gaudry-Schost for the Cartier-Manin operator). If the interval polynomial F_m(X) = prod (X - x(jP)) were such a product, then membership "x(Q) in x([a,b]P)" and the whole BSGS table could be replaced by an O~(sqrt m) evaluation, giving an algebraic S/T trade-off outside the usual table accounting.
Gate: needs a P-recursive recurrence with polynomial coefficients in k over F_p. The exponent group is Z/r, the polynomial index group is Z/p; translation by P is not a linear map on any fixed space of functions (L(nO) is not translation-invariant), so no mechanism for it exists unless r = p, the anomalous case (Smart; HYP-93fbd7d0 checked the Hensel digits, not the x-sequence). W-GEN does not apply (this is an algebraic, non-generic claim); W-INT does not apply (degree bounds are in x, not k). Survives as a test.
Test (E2): guess sum_{i<=d} c_i(k) u_{k+i} = 0, 1 <= d <= 6, deg c_i <= e <= 6, with 60 spare equations, for u = x(kP), y(kP) and prod_{j<=m}(X0 - x(jP)) on 4 curves (14 and 18 bits, 2 each). Positive controls found (k! and Catalan: nullity 1 at d=e=1, as expected). No recurrence for any real sequence in the grid (0/12 sequence-curve pairs). **Killed by test, wall: index-group mismatch Z/r vs F_p (only anomalous exception).** Limit: grid d,e <= 6 only.

**C3. Tensor / displacement structure of the Cayley table and a group-convolution oracle in O~(r).**
Mechanism: N_AB = x(A+B) + x(A-B) = 2[(x_A+x_B)(x_A x_B + a) + 2b]/(x_A - x_B)^2, so D o N with D_AB = (x_A - x_B)^2 has rank <= 3 (a Cauchy-like matrix of displacement rank 3 in the second-order operator). Matrix-vector products with the symmetrised convolution operator on E then cost O~(r) via multipoint evaluation of rational functions, without knowing any logarithm; one hopes for Krylov/Lanczos spectral access to the characters.
Gate: (i) the identity is just the biquadratic addition law (Semaev S_3 / x-line correspondence), not new; (ii) every object here is a vector indexed by all of E(F_p): listing x-labels already costs >= r (W-TAB), above sqrt r, and the output size of the convolution is r; (iii) Krylov vectors from delta_Q under the translation operator have translation-invariant Lanczos coefficients, so they carry no information on Q. **Killed on paper by wall W-TAB (+ no information, cf. W-GEN spirit).**
Exact check (E3a): the identity holds entrywise and rank_Fp(D o N) = 3 for 57x57 (p=211) and 120x120 (p=1009) submatrices of distinct-x pairs, while the unscaled N has full rank (57, 120). This verifies the algebra only; it is not a cost test.

**C4. Dequantised Shor / holographic (tensor-network) representation of the state sum |a>|aP>.**
Mechanism: simulate period finding classically by compressing the state in a matrix-product form and sampling the Fourier distribution, with an exact bond-dimension cost model (Tang-style dequantisation needs low rank).
Gate: for an injective map a -> aP the matrix of the cut (low j bits | rest, including the target register) has orthogonal rows with equal norm: flat singular spectrum, rank 2^j, and the cut a | target has rank 2^n >= r/2 with all singular values equal. Truncation to bond chi has fidelity chi/rank exactly, so there is no compressible spectrum; contraction cost >= r^(omega) > sqrt r. Injectivity makes this a restatement of "the oracle is a bijection" (W-GEN spirit and W-TAB).
Test (E3b, r = 137 and 1013): spectra flat at every cut, ranks 2^j (j = 1..n), a|target rank 128 and 512. **Killed (exact linear algebra), wall W-TAB.**

**C5. CRT in the unit group Z/r^*: multiplicative-character leak with self-reduction by known multipliers.**
Mechanism: instead of additive structure (k mod m, bits, MSB; closed in rounds 4, 12, 20) use the multiplicative structure of the exponent. Write k = g^i with g a generator of (Z/r)^*; for q | r-1 the class c_q(k) = i mod q is a q-th-power-residue character of k. Self-reduction: c_q(mk) = c_q(m) + c_q(k), and mQ is computed from Q for any known m, so an oracle that predicts c_q(k) from the point with advantage eps > 0 can be re-randomised and majority-voted (about 2 ln(2q/delta)/eps^2 votes, each one scalar multiplication). Pohlig-Hellman in the unit group then assembles i mod prod q; the final search (baby-step giant-step over k = g^i, using only scalar multiples of known points) runs on r/prod q candidates. For r-1 smooth (every prime power of r-1 covered) this is a polynomial-time DLP; for generic r-1 the gain is a constant. Dynamics view: the Lattes maps L_n on the x-line act as k -> nk, so L_g orbits are exactly the unit-group cycle and carry zero information on k by themselves (cycle type depends only on r); Q is located in that orbit only by a character-type oracle.
Gate: (a) Algebraic version (compute x(k^e P) from x(kP) by interpolation) is excluded by Theorem B (e = 2: deg >= (r-5)/6) and by the absence of a Diffie-Hellman oracle: this is the known Maurer-Wolf / den Boer DL-from-DH reduction, which needs a DH oracle and an auxiliary group of smooth order, so the "CRT via unit-group Pohlig-Hellman" idea is not new in structure (recollection, unverified). (b) Statistical version (a predictor of c_q(k) with advantage eps from low-complexity features of Q and its neighbours): not excluded by Theorems B/C/DT, but bounded by W-LEAK. Derived bar (unreviewed; same-implementation rho fit below): total cost >= sum_q 2 ln(2q/delta) c_v / eps^2 + beta sqrt(r/prod q) must be below alpha sqrt r + gamma. With 20 primes (r-1 smooth) the break-even advantage is eps* ~ 0.0026 at r = 2^64 and ~0.13 at 2^40; no eps < 1 breaks even at 2^20 (toy sizes) for either set; for the tested set S = {2,3,5} the maximal possible gain is sqrt(30) = 5.5 (a constant). (c) Prior work: hard-core-bit results for DL (Hastad-Naslund; Boneh-Venkatesan, Jetchev-Venkatesan for ECDH bits) suggest hardness; unverified recollection, not consulted. Survives paper gate in the statistical version, so it was tested.
Test (E4, corrected analysis, 6 curves at 16/18/20 bits, two each, r = 1 mod 30, units k = 1..(r-1)/2, 236 low-complexity features of Q, x/y of Q +- P, +- 2P, [2]Q, [3]Q, [5]Q and relative features, q in {2,3,5}, 708 tests per curve, Bonferroni 0.01, label-permutation max-T check, cross-fitted held-out accuracy): 0/6 curves with a Bonferroni-significant real feature (smallest p 9.4e-4 against threshold 1.4e-5); permutation max-T p-values 0.16-1.0 on all 18 (curve, q) cells; best held-out gain (selected over 708 tests, not corrected for selection) 0.013 at 16 bits and 0.0027-0.0053 at 18-20 bits, with 99% upper bounds 0.023 (16 bits), 0.0092 (18 bits), 0.0055 (20 bits). Planted c_2 leak of advantage 0.05 flagged on all 6 curves (held-out gain 0.048-0.051, lower bounds 0.041-0.048; p <= 1e-39). For comparison r^(-1/4) = 0.031 at 2^20. **No leak; the statistical bar is not even approached at these sizes. Killed at toy scale (not for larger r or other feature families).**
Protocol defect recorded (Addendum 1): the first E4 run (16 bits) used the registered chi-square on all k = 1..r-1 and flagged 32 and 20 "significant" x-based tests; this is an artifact because kP and (r-k)P share x and (for odd q) the same class, a design effect of 2 (identical p-values for x, x[2]Q, x[3]Q, x[5]Q from the class relabelling c_q(mk) = c_q(m) + c_q(k)). The planted control was flagged in that run too, so it could not detect the flaw. The invalid output is kept in aborted/ and not used. This was a pre-registration error of mine, found because four features gave bit-identical p-values.

**C6. Reed-Solomon list decoding of the DL codeword (Guruswami-Sudan window).**
Mechanism: the word (j)_{j<=N} on evaluation points x(jP) is a Reed-Solomon received word. If some polynomial g of degree d agrees with it (g(x(jP)) = j) in t > sqrt(N d) places, Guruswami-Sudan list decoding finds g in polynomial time, and Horner (cost d) then gives k on a t/N fraction of re-randomised queries Q + jP.
Gate: the lab's partial-agreement bound (STATUS round 7/8; rational-function version, proof attempt, unreviewed) gives d >= delta^2 N/4 with delta = t/N, i.e. t <= 2 sqrt(N d): the existence window sqrt(Nd) < t <= 2 sqrt(Nd) is not excluded by W-INT (arithmetic is a PI-level derivation, unreviewed). Expected cost per solved instance with a cost-c(d) evaluator is c(d) N/t >= c(d) sqrt(N)/(2 sqrt d), which for an SLP-evaluated g of low evaluation cost could be below sqrt N, but for a plain degree-d polynomial (c = d) requires d <= 3. So the gate only leaves high-degree polynomials with short SLPs, for which only weak SLP lower bounds are proved (log-type, Theorem section addendum). Random-word counting predicts agreement t ~ d + 2 (no excess). Survives the paper gate narrowly; tested in the cheapest form.
Test (E5, d = 1 only): for each curve, largest number of other points collinear with a pivot in {(x(jP), j)} and {(x(jP), j^2 mod p)}, j <= 1200, versus 20 label permutations; planted 30% affine alignment detected at 337-379 of expected 325-379 on all 12 cells. Real data at 3-4 collinear partners versus null maximum 3-4 on 6 curves (bits 16, 18); rule (exceeds null maximum on >= 4 of 6 curves for both targets) not met (1 of 12 cells exceeded by one unit). **Null at d = 1; d >= 2 and the Guruswami-Sudan decoder itself were NOT implemented (stated limit); not closed for high-degree short-SLP g.**

**C7. Product-tree / resultant BSGS: replace the hash table by the polynomial F_T(X) = prod_{j<=T}(X - x(jP)) and answer M giant steps by multipoint evaluation or Res(F_T, H_M) in O~(T + M).**
Mechanism: algebraic precomputation (S = T coefficients) plus one product tree for many membership queries.
Gate: the lists still need M giant-step points x(Q - iTP) (one group operation each, Theorem DT: information about k enters only through group operations and exact collisions), and T baby steps, so total group operations are T + M with T M >= r, i.e. >= 2 sqrt r; polynomial arithmetic only replaces an O(1) hash lookup by polylog factors. With preprocessing the accounting is S T^2 >= r (W-ST2, generic scope; the polynomial route is non-generic but needs the lists). Making the giant-step polynomial H_M(X) = prod_i (X - x(Q+iR)) without computing points is exactly C2 (needs P-recursivity), which failed. **Killed on paper by W-ST2 / Theorem DT accounting + C2.** Not tested separately.

**C8. Greedy navigation (Kleinberg-style) on the Cayley cycle with long-range links.**
Mechanism: E(F_p) with edges Q ~ Q +- P is a cycle graph of length r; DLP is graph distance. Add long-range links (jumps 2^i P) of known length and route greedily toward the target.
Gate: greedy routing needs an embedding whose geometry predicts graph distance, i.e. a coordinate f with |f(x(Q)) - k| = o(r) in a measurable fraction: that is the MSB/regression leak of round 20 (null, bar r^(-1/4)) and the near-character search (HYP-130de9b1, null). Without geometry the shortcuts reduce to BSGS/rho step choices (rounds 12, 16). **Killed on paper by W-LEAK, using round-20 data; not retested.**

## Claims and verification
Everything above is experiment at toy size (bits 10-20, E6 to 24) or an unreviewed paper derivation; no formal proof, no review, no independent replication. Limits: gain/selection over 708 tests not corrected for the reported best gain; 6 curves only; one feature family; E2 grid d, e <= 6; E5 d = 1 only; E6 CI wide.
